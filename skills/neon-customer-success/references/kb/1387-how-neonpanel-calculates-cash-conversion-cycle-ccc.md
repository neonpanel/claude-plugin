# How NeonPanel Calculates Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)

Category: Inventory Management · Article 1387 · Updated 2026-02-25

NeonPanel calculates the Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) by analyzing sold items, their associated PO batches, payment installments, and inventory costs to determine Days Payable Outstanding (DPO), Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO), and Days Sales Outstanding (DSO). This automated calculation provides precise insights into cash flow efficiency, helping businesses optimize operations and financial planning.

## Introduction (Part 1)

How NeonPanel Calculates Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) Understanding your business’s Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) is crucial for managing cash flow effectively. In NeonPanel, we automate the CCC calculation by analyzing your sales, inventory, and payment data, ensuring accurate insights tailored to your operations. Here’s how we calculate CCC: 1. Analyzing Sold Items by Invoice Date We begin by analyzing all sold items using the invoice date as the reference point. Each sold item is linked to its respective Purchase Order (PO) batch, allowing us to track its journey from procurement to sale. 2. Identifying PO Batches and Their Details For every PO batch associated with a sold item, NeonPanel records: • Order Date: The date the PO was placed with the supplier. • Payment Installments: Each installment’s amount and date. • Received Date: The date the order was received in your inventory. This comprehensive data enables us to understand the timing and flow of payments and inventory. 3. Calculating Days Payable Outstanding (DPO) To calculate DPO, we use a weighted algorithm that factors in: • The amount of each payment installment. • The timing of each payment relative to the order date. This approach provides a precise measurement of how long your business takes to pay suppliers. 4. Calculating Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO) DIO reflects how efficiently you manage inventory.
