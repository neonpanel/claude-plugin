# Inventory Related Accounting Transactions

Category: Accounting · Article 1360 · Updated 2026-02-25

This article outlines two scenarios for managing purchase orders in NeonPanel: one using the In Transit account and one where inventory is received directly. It covers key financial steps, including deposits, order completion, shipment receipt, and landed cost allocation, ensuring accurate cost and payment tracking.

## Introduction

NeonPanel allows users to set up asset accounts based on their accounting policies. There are various methods discussed in this article, and each can be operationalized using the Transaction Map page in NeonPanel.

However, as a general guideline, we recommend using three primary asset accounts to track inventory purchases: Inventory Assets, In Transit, and In Fabrication. While the account names may differ depending on your company’s specific approach, the underlying structure remains consistent. Below are two common scenarios for managing purchase orders (POs) and the related financial transactions.

Note: The transactions involving your bank account currently happen outside NP via your accounting software which is connected to your bank.

## Scenario 1: Using the In Transit Account (Part 1)

Overview:In this scenario, a business places an order worth $10,000, pays a deposit of $2,500, and once the order is complete, the goods are sent via one or multiple shipments. During the shipping process, freight and other landed costs are incurred and capitalized into the inventory value. After all goods are received, the remaining balance of $7,500 is paid, and the business settles the bill for the landed costs. Additionally, when the shipment is received, the landed costs are transferred into the Inventory Assets account. ## Scenario 2: Not Using the In Transit Account (Part 1)

Overview:In this scenario, a business places an order worth $10,000 and pays a $2,500 deposit. Once the order is complete, the goods are received directly into inventory without using the In Transit account. Freight and other landed costs are capitalized directly into the inventory upon receipt. After receiving the goods, the remaining balance of $7,500 is paid, and the business also settles the bill for the landed costs. Upon receipt of the shipment, the landed costs are transferred into the Inventory Assets account.
