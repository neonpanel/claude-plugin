# COGS & Income Variations

Category: Contextuals · Article 774 · Updated 2026-02-25

NeonPanel provides multiple methods of calculating COGS. Understanding these variations and their applications can provide valuable insights into your business operations.

## Introduction

The Profit Analytics dashboard features a COGS Variations tab that offers different methods of calculating COGS, enabling you to gain a better understanding of your business.

The variance in COGS under each method will impact your seller discretionary earnings (SDE) metric. Therefore, the dashboard also includes an SDE Variations tab.

Let's take a closer look at each COGS calculation method.

## Average COGS

This method sums up all the COGS associated with your products across all inventory orders within a specified date range and divides them by the total units ordered.

## FIFO COGS

Here, COGS is calculated based on the first-in-first-out (FIFO) accounting method. Whenever a unit is sold, the COGS from the earliest still available inventory order are expensed.

## Average LTM3 COGS

This method calculates the COGS for the past twelve months, minus the most recent three months. It assumes that you primarily sell products purchased three months ago.

For instance, if you're preparing a report in January 2023, your COGS would be the average landed cost for the period of November 1, 2021, to October 31, 2022.

## Last Received COGS

This method calculates the COGS of the most recent inventory order marked as received and still available in stock. This amount is used as a constant for the entire analysis period.

It answers the question: what would be my profit for that period if my COGS were the same as my most recent order?

## Current COGS

This variation is a figure that you can calculate when you receive updated pricing information from your vendor or learn of new freight costs, for example.

This data is stored in the Inventory Catalog, and you will need to manually update it since NeonPanel has no way of knowing about these costs until you provide them.

The Current COGS is an essential factor to consider when evaluating potential profits, as it allows you to determine what profit you could make with a new price.

If you plan to sell your business, using Current COGS can be a valuable tool for demonstrating forecasted profits to potential buyers.
