# NP Catalogs: Service Groups

Category: Catalogs · Article 1202 · Updated 2026-02-25

Learn how to effectively manage and allocate costs in NeonPanel using our guide on service groups.

## Why Service Groups are Essential

Service Groups in NeonPanel streamline managing service allocation algorithms. Creating specific groups with designated allocations allows users to easily connect new services, ensuring consistency and efficiency.

## Introduction

This guide is for bookkeepers, accountants, and users managing cost data in NeonPanel. Service Groups help allocate expenses and costs to each inventory item, crucial for determining item-specific profit or loss. NeonPanel’s flexible system allows businesses to customize cost labeling to fit their needs.

## Main Service Group Types

1. Expenses: Regular costs like advertising, storage, or FBA fees.

2. Landed Costs: Costs for moving products from the supplier to the warehouse, including customs fees.

3. Assets: For Amazon sellers, this primarily includes inventory, the main asset being the products they sell.

## How to Create Service Groups

1. Navigate to Service Groups: Go to 'Service Groups' in NeonPanel and click 'Add new Service Group'.

2. Name and Type: Provide a name and select a group type (e.g., 'Inventory Shipping' as 'Landed Cost').

3. Allocation Base and Scope: Choose how NeonPanel should handle each cost added. Scopes include shipment, PO, warehouse, brand, marketplace, company, product family, parent ASIN, and ASIN.

## How to Use Service Groups

The allocation base determines how costs are distributed among items. For example, shipping costs might be split based on weight or size, while customs fees might use item value. After setting up service groups, link them to the appropriate service items in your 'Services' list.

## Important Notes:

- Landed Cost Services: Allocation base and scope are crucial for these services. Expenses are capitalized and distributed among inventory items, requiring precise allocation.

- Non-Landed Cost Services: Typically connected to a service group called 'Other Expenses'. These services are linked directly to accounts for journal entries and do not require distribution among inventory items. They go directly on the P&L (Profit and Loss) statement.

## Example

For instance, a 'Customs' service item can be linked to a group named 'Customs Duty (Landed Cost)', with 'Inventory Order' as its scope and 'Customs Duty' as its base. This ensures costs are correctly allocated to each item based on duty percentages for each item.
