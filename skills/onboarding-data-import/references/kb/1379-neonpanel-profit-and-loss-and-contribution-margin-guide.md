# NeonPanel Profit and Loss and Contribution Margin Guide

Category: Accounting · Article 1379 · Updated 2026-02-25

NeonPanel’s Profit and Loss report provides a detailed breakdown of profitability through Contribution Margins (CMs): CM1 (Gross Margin) reflects profitability after inventory costs (landed costs), CM2 (Operating Margin) accounts for additional direct selling costs (e.g., fulfillment, packaging, storage), and CM3 (Net Profit) factors in operational expenses (e.g., advertising, salaries, software). This structured approach ensures precise financial insights, helping businesses measure performance at every level while excluding non-operational costs like extraordinary or financial expenses from core metrics.

## Introduction

NeonPanel uses a detailed approach to calculate contribution margins (CM) to help businesses measure profitability accurately at various levels. This guide explains the calculation of CM1, CM2, and CM3, their significance, and how to map accounts in QBO and Xero for clarity and precision.

Using the “Other Expenses” Account Type

The “Other Expenses” account type is used for costs that are outside core business operations and do not impact CM3 calculations. These are extraordinary or non-operational expenses that are unrelated to day-to-day profitability.

Examples of “Other Expenses”:

•    Non-Operational Costs: Loss on asset disposal, legal settlements, charitable contributions.

•    Extraordinary Costs: Natural disaster recovery, uninsured losses, write-offs.

•    Financial Expenses: Interest payments, unrealized currency exchange losses.

Why Exclude “Other Expenses” from CM3?

CM3 reflects the net profitability of core operations. Including one-off or non-operational expenses could distort this metric, reducing its value for analyzing business performance.

Best Practices for “Other Expenses”

1.    Categorize Thoughtfully: Ensure only non-operational costs are included here.

2.    Report Separately: Include “Other Expenses” in financial summaries, but keep them outside CM3 calculations.

3.    Provide Context: Document the reasons for classification to avoid confusion during audits.

By clearly distinguishing between operational and non-operational costs, NeonPanel users can maintain accurate, actionable financial insights to drive their businesses forward.
