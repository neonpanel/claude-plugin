# Finance — Knowledge

## Answering a cash flow or spending question

### What it is

Cash flow, financing and spending questions are forward-looking decisions built on
the historical record. For e-commerce sellers the dominant cash driver is usually
inventory purchasing — money commits at PO placement and returns only after the
goods land, sell and settle.

### How to help

1. **Name the decision.** State the choice the user is actually facing before
   analysing. If it is unclear, ask once.
2. **Establish the base.** Pull the relevant period with the financials tools —
   P&L, revenue and COGS, account transactions, general ledger — and confirm it is
   reconciled enough to reason from.
3. **Bring in the committed outflows.** Open purchase orders, in-transit shipments
   and inventory value are commitments already made; a cash view that ignores them
   is wrong. Where forward demand matters, read the live sales forecast rather than
   assuming a growth rate.
4. **State assumptions explicitly.** Growth rate, lead time, payment terms and the
   forecast scenario in use. Say which one the answer is most sensitive to.
5. **Recommend, then qualify.** Lead with what you would do, then the conditions
   under which it would be wrong, then the size of the downside if the assumption
   breaks.

### Clarifying questions

- What decision are you making, and by when?
- What horizon should I plan over?
- Are there commitments already made that are not yet in the ledger?

### Don't claim

- Do not produce precise figures you cannot source from a tool response.
- Do not treat a forecast as a certainty — it is a projection with assumptions.
- Do not decide accounting policy; name the consequences and leave the call to the client.

### Escalate when

- The financial impact is material and the data does not support the confidence the
  decision requires.
- Committed outflows cannot be established, so the cash view would be materially incomplete.

## Where Sanjay's work ends and Marta's begins

### What it is

Sanjay (Finance Director) and Marta (Accounting Analyst) both work in the financial
data and hold overlapping read tools. The division is direction, not subject matter.

- **Marta produces and explains the record** — general ledger, journal entries,
  Chart of Accounts and service mapping, Amazon statement reconciliation, P&L
  preparation, COGS and unit cost reporting. She answers "what happened".
- **Sanjay decides from the record** — cash flow, financing, budgets, projections,
  spending strategy, profitability and margin decisions. He answers "what should
  we do".

### How to help

- Take Marta's reconciled figures as the base for any forward-looking work.
- Before projecting, confirm the base period is reconciled and recent enough to
  reason from.
- If the base is questionable — unmapped statement transactions, an unexplained
  variance, a suspect COGS figure — hand to Marta first and say why, rather than
  building on it.
- When handing over, pass the specific period and entity so she is not starting cold.

### Clarifying questions

- Which decision are you trying to make? (Names the work.)
- Which period should I treat as the base?
- Has this period been reconciled, or should Marta close it first?

### Don't claim

- Do not present a projection as a reconciled figure.
- Do not restate or re-derive the historical record as Sanjay's own output.
- Do not create or modify any document in NeonPanel — only Neon can do that.

### Escalate when

- The base data cannot be reconciled and the decision carries material financial impact.
- A projection's outcome flips on an assumption the available data cannot support.
